A personal career & work hub

Your career, your work and what you know — one record, seen four ways.

Mashghal holds the professional record behind everything you do: the roles and training that make up a career, the projects and workflows that are the work, and the scripts, formulas, methods and references you keep reaching for. Then it lets you read that same record as a portfolio, a timeline, or a CV written for one particular reader.

The shape of it

Three worlds, and one record underneath them

Most tools make you pick: a task app, a notes app, a CV builder, a reference manager. Mashghal is built the other way round — one professional record, with the screens as different questions asked of it.

Career

Who I am professionally

The half of your working life that outlives any one job, and the half a CV is made of.

  • Profile — the one place the app asks about you
  • Experience — roles, and the employers that hold them
  • Education and Training, treated as equals
  • Skills, each with its evidence under it
  • Timeline, derived from dates you already typed

Work

What I am doing and have done

The operational layer — and it sits under the career rather than beside it, so a project is both live work and evidence of experience.

  • Projects, naming the employer and the role they ran under
  • Workflows written once and run many times
  • Actions — late, today, in hand, waiting
  • People, Equipment, Outputs, Employers
  • Hours, summed from entries and never stored as a total

Knowledge

What I know and can reuse

Not a notes app. The things you have already worked out and will need again, with the content itself — not a link to where it might be.

  • Scripts — Python, ArcPy, GDAL, SQL, PowerShell
  • Formulas — the spreadsheet ones you rebuild every year
  • Methods and Templates, written out as prose
  • References, as real citation records
  • Places — a folder, a file, a link, a shortcut

And across all three

Portfolio and CVs are selections, never copies

A portfolio item and a CV hold no facts of their own. They point at records that already exist — so correcting a date once corrects it everywhere, and a CV cannot quietly describe a version of your career the rest of the app has stopped agreeing with. Several CVs can exist at once, each aimed at one reader.

The connective tissue

One relationship layer, and every reverse is worked out

Seven kinds of line: uses, taught, is about, shows, includes, cites, involved. Each is stated once, in one direction, and the other direction is derived — so the two can never disagree about what a record is connected to.

The library

The things you already worked out, kept where you will look

Six months later you remember what the script did, not what you called it or which folder it went in. So the library holds the content itself — the code, the formula, the method written out — and it is searched by its body and by the subjects you write onto it, not only by its name.

Script

Code you reuse

Python, ArcPy, GDAL, SQL, PowerShell. Leading whitespace is kept exactly, because it is what a Python block means — only the trailing blank lines a textarea collects come off.

Formula

The spreadsheet ones

Written out with what it is for, so “Area of a polygon from a coordinate list” reads better in six months than “formula 3”.

Method

A technique, in prose

Code does not wrap and prose does: a method is set to be read, so it wraps rather than scrolling sideways at its first long sentence.

Template

A skeleton to start from

A report outline, a metadata sheet, a naming rule. The thing you copy at the beginning of every job of that kind.

Reference

Something to cite

A real citation record — eighteen fields, eleven types — because a bibliography cannot be assembled out of prose. See below.

Place

Where something lives

A folder, a file, a link, a shortcut. One kind, because all four are the same act: something you open. And where a browser is not allowed to open a local path, the screen says so instead of drawing a button that does nothing.

The bibliography

References come in whole, and go out in five formats

Typing eighteen fields per paper is how a bibliography feature goes unused, so paste the BibTeX or RIS that every catalogue, Zotero and Google Scholar will hand you — several at once. It is read here, character by character, with nothing fetched and nothing looked up. What it cannot place, it names rather than dropping.

Five formatsA plain author–date list for pasting into what you are writing now · BibTeX for LaTeX, Overleaf, JabRef · RIS for EndNote, Mendeley, Papers · CSL‑JSON for anything that formats a named style properly · and just the cite keys, one per line, for the \cite{…} handles.
Five scopesEvery reference · everything on one subject · everything used on one project · everything about one skill · and everything cited by one output — which is the one you actually want when a paper is going out.
Honest about gapsA reference short of an author, a year or a title is named on the page and in the exported file. A bibliography entry with a hole in it is worse than a missing one, so nothing goes out half-formed without saying so.

Evidence, not claims

A skill is a word until something in the record backs it

Nothing here computes a proficiency out of how many projects used a word. A level is typed or it is blank — and what the app works out instead is the evidence: which training taught it, which projects used it, which workflows apply it, which outputs demonstrate it. That is checkable. A number is not.

Ask it in words

A box you can ask a question, with no model behind it

Ctrl K searches the record — every name, every body of code, every subject, every citation field. End a sentence with a question mark, or start it with a question word, and it is read as a question instead: matched by pattern and answered through the same function the screen it points at uses. It says which question it thought it heard, and when it cannot shape one it prints the list of what it can be asked rather than half-answering.

what needs meLate first, because that is the only thing that cannot wait.

On a phone

The same record, one column, and no chrome to spare

Measured at 390, 360 and 320 px: five tabs on one row, nothing scrolled off the edge, and the whole of the header — mark, name, search and settings — on a single line. A screen that small cannot afford a fourth row of furniture.

It installs as an app if you want it to — it carries its own manifest and its own offline shell, so it opens on a plane and on a train. The one thing it will not do is pretend to notify you: a browser cannot send mail, and nothing runs while the app is shut, so what is late is waiting when you open it.

Also measured: every run of text in both themes clears the 4.5:1 contrast floor, at rest and under the pointer; and every control takes the app’s own focus ring, so the whole thing can be driven from a keyboard.

Getting started

Four steps, and then it is your record

There is a full guide inside the app — the gear in the corner, then How to use Mashghal, or just search for guide. It walks every record type, in the order that makes the later ones worth having.

Look at the example first

On the empty screen, press Load the example. It is one archaeologist’s book with every part of the app in it — projects, workflows, a bibliography, two CVs. Read it, then erase it in Settings and start your own. Nothing is invented for you otherwise: an empty book stays empty until you type.

Write your profile and one role

A name and one line about what you do. Then Career → Experience → New role, with the employer if it has one — a thesis, a fellowship or freelance work belongs to none, and that is a real answer.

Add a project, and hang the work off it

Work → Projects. Name the role it runs under: that one field is what makes the same record live work here and a line of experience on the career side. Then its actions, its outputs, the workflow you run on it.

Draw the lines — that is the whole point

Link it to… is on every record’s own page. A project uses a skill; training taught it; an output demonstrates it; a portfolio item shows the output. Four taps, and a word on a list has become something you can put in front of somebody.

Honest limits

What it deliberately does not do

Four things, each for a reason worth stating rather than leaving as a gap you discover later.

It does not score you

No streaks, no grades, no productivity number. The one percentage anywhere in the app is a count of actions you marked done, and it says so where it is printed.

It does not infer a skill level

A level is typed or blank. What is derived is the evidence — which records back the claim — because that is checkable and a number is not.

It does not notify

A browser cannot send mail and nothing runs while the app is shut. What is late is waiting when you open it. Implying otherwise would be worse than the limit.

It fetches nothing

No fonts, no libraries, no service, no analytics. Which is why it opens with no connection — and why the bibliography is formatted here rather than by asking somebody else’s server to do it.

Where is my data?
In your browser, under one key, on the machine you typed it on. There is no account and no server in this build, so nothing is uploaded and nothing is shared — and the honest other half of that is that a backup file is the only way it reaches a second machine. Settings has Back it up and Restore a backup side by side for exactly that.
Can I use it on my laptop and my phone?
Yes, but they hold separate books in this build. Back up on one and restore on the other when you want them to match. There is no sync, and the app says so rather than implying there is.
What happens if I clear my browser data?
The book goes with it, the same as any site’s storage. Back it up first. Settings will hand you the whole record as a file you can read and keep anywhere.
Does it work offline?
That is the point. Open it once with a connection and it keeps its own offline copy; after that it opens with no network at all. It can be installed as an app from your browser’s menu if you would rather it had its own window.
Is the bibliography a real citation style?
The four machine formats are exact: BibTeX, RIS, CSL-JSON and the cite keys go straight into LaTeX, Zotero, EndNote or Mendeley. The human-readable list is author–date and says so in its own header — it is not an implementation of APA, MLA, Chicago or Harvard, because a hand-rolled named style would be a plausible wrong answer, and that is worse than a visible limit. Export CSL-JSON and let a reference manager format the named style properly.
Is this for archaeologists only?
No. The example happens to be an archaeologist’s, because the app was built for one — but nothing in the model knows what your work is. The kinds of thing (a project’s status, an output’s kind, a skill’s group, an equipment type) are all words you type, and the app never has to understand them.
What does “Mashghal” mean?
مشغل — a workshop. A bench per craft, and the record of what was made at each one.
